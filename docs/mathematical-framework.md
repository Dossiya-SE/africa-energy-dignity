# Mathematical Framework

## Purpose

Translate energy dignity and rapid deployment into a transparent, testable and reproducible decision model.

## Decision vector

The decision vector may include:

- Generation capacity by technology and location
- Storage power and energy capacity
- Grid-extension and reinforcement decisions
- Mini-grid and stand-alone assignments
- Productive-use equipment allocation
- Construction and commissioning sequence
- Workforce and local-content allocation

## Objective families

### Minimize

- Lifecycle cost
- Deployment time
- Lifecycle environmental impact
- Unserved energy
- External dependency
- Expected service loss
- Implementation risk

### Maximize

- Reliability
- Productive capability
- Local value creation
- Energy sovereignty
- Social benefit
- Resilience
- Deployment readiness

## Scientific safeguards

- Normalize incomparable objectives before weighted aggregation.
- Publish all weights.
- Test sensitivity to weights.
- Prefer Pareto-front analysis when a single weighted score would conceal tradeoffs.
- Report infeasible scenarios rather than forcing a numerical answer.
- Separate input uncertainty, parameter uncertainty and structural uncertainty.
